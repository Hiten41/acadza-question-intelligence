# Product judgment

**1. Who loses the most when a question is broken, and how does the design reflect that?**

The student who trusts the answer key while preparing for a high-stakes exam bears the immediate cost: lost study time and reduced confidence. Faculty also lose time when duplicate reports arrive without the source question or a clear calculation. The console keeps every student's original report attached to one question case and puts the question, solution, answer, evidence, and exact proposed edit beside each other so a reviewer can decide quickly.

**2. What would you never let the AI auto-apply, and why?**

I would never let an uncertain answer-key change, an invented option or diagram, an unsound question, or a syllabus/chapter decision publish without faculty approval. Those choices affect what students learn and cannot be safely inferred from a complaint alone. In this demo, only high-confidence, taxonomy-permitted edits are marked as safe candidates; they are staged locally and never touch a production record.

**3. What one metric would you watch in week one?**

Track the median time from a report entering the queue to a faculty decision, segmented by whether the case required a content correction. Pair it with a spot check of approved edits: faster review is useful only if students are not receiving incorrect fixes.

**4. What is one idea beyond the assignment that you would pitch to Acadza?**

Create a student-facing correction receipt when a verified issue is fixed: show what changed, when it changed, and whether a saved practice result should be re-scored. That closes the loop for the learners whose reports improve the question bank.
